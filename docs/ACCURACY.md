# Accuracy

All numbers are relative L2 errors of GPU readback against the exact Liénard–Wiechert field with **no softening**, generated by `tests/gpu/tables.mjs` (raw data: [results/tables.json](../results/tables.json)). Renderer: ANGLE (Google, Vulkan 1.3.0 (SwiftShader Device (Subzero) (0x0000C0DE)), SwiftShader driver). Software rendering is IEEE float32 but slow; real GPUs should be checked with the in-app suite.

Numbers after v6.0.0 include the κ-form radiation term and the Newton fix; preprint v6 corresponds to tag v6.0.0 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23601496).

Sections 1–6 are the **v6** kernel (`V6_SHADERS`, the app's *Kernel: v6*), which v6.2 leaves bit-identical. Since v6.2 the default kernel of the app and the library is **v6f**; section 7 describes it, corrects the attribution of v6's remaining error at γ ≥ 71, and gives both kernels side by side ([results/table2_v6_2.tsv](../results/table2_v6_2.tsv)).

**Dragged (live) histories.** The indicator also has acceleration terms for user-driven motion. The first is a jump in the stored acceleration, seen as the largest second difference over the Catmull–Rom support; the cubic smooths such a jump across its light sphere. The second is stored acceleration that is inconsistent with the change of stored velocity across the segment. Each is converted to an estimated radiation-field error relative to the source's own field. Below β = 0.2 the bend term's 1/β² is floored, because it flagged charges at rest. The measurements below come from the app's drag rules (velocity relaxing toward the pointer, |Δβ| per step limited, acceleration derived from the actual velocity change) on a pointer path with jumps, holds, rapid reversals and a fast circle: 3 snapshots, 96 × 96 observers over [−3, 3]², exact field from the recorded history. *Recall* is the share of observers with error > 1% that the mask flags; the tuning scripts were development scripts and are not included; tests/gpu/drag_mask.mjs is a reduced version of them.

| M | observers with error > 1% | recall before | recall now | flagged before → now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 128 | 24% | 35% | 99% | 23% → 56% |
| 512 | 4.8% | 0% | 96% | 2.6% → 12% |
| 2048 | 0.4% | 0% | 99% | 0.5% → 8.8% |

With the mask on, the 99th-percentile error of unflagged observers on these histories is below 1% at every M (0.9%, 0.4%, 0.05%). Accurate observers near a charge at rest are no longer flagged (35% → 11% at M = 128, 24% → 0% at M = 512). The field values themselves are unchanged; only the indicator is.

## 6. Known-path mode

When a source follows a preset whose position, velocity, acceleration and jerk are known in closed form, its shells can be filled with exact samples of the path instead of resampling the ring buffer. Segments between two exact knots use a quintic Hermite (position, velocity and acceleration matched at both knots; quartic hodograph) with the |z′|² certificate extended to degree 8. Where the certificate does not limit the segment (α = 1) the curve is C2; a limited segment (α < 1) no longer matches the endpoint derivatives and is C0 there, as the cubic is. Acceleration is a cubic Hermite in the stored jerk; |u| is a cubic Hermite only where the segment is resolved (bend term of the validity indicator < 1), else linear as before. Knots whose retarded time falls in a stretch where the user dragged the source come from the ring buffer as before, and so do live (dragged-only) sources. It is a separate shader program (`#define KNOWN_PATH`): the ring program is unchanged.

One source on the circular orbit (radius 0.85), no softening, float32 readback in software rendering. *sweep* = the pulse sweep of section 1; *view* = 24 × 24 observers over the app's default view (points within 0.05 of the orbit excluded); *far* = 512 observers at 6 < R < 20 from the orbit centre. At γ ≈ 224 the sweep uses double-precision observer coordinates. Far-field figures at small M are dominated by observers outside the shells' reach (the validity indicator marks them).

**Grab and release.** Orbit preset (β = 0.8, 4 sources, M = 512): source 0 dragged on a jerky path for 500 steps, released, and left to rejoin its orbit; the same run in ring mode for comparison. Both modes rejoin at the same step (1102 / 1102). On a 12 × 12 grid the two fields differ by at most 3.3e-6 RMS (1.0e-4 worst point) at every snapshot through drag, return and rejoin, so the hand-off adds no visible step. Exact knots return as the dragged stretch moves outward: 505 of 512 before the grab, 55 at the fewest, 493 at the last snapshot.

**Auto shell count.** The app's M_T button has an *Auto* setting: it picks M from the view's largest retarded radius (view diagonal + 0.9) — 128 when that is ≤ 6 and γ ≤ 100, 512 when it is ≤ 6 at higher γ or ≤ 20, else 2048. The app's view is fixed at |y| ≤ 1.45, so Auto gives 128 shells (512 above γ = 100). The thresholds follow the equal-accuracy table above and the far-field qualification (the saving is ≤ 4× by R ≈ 20); they assume known-path mode, and in ring mode Auto is less accurate than a fixed 512 or 2048.

Not done: closed-form fields for uniform and hyperbolic motion (the app has no hyperbolic preset, and its straight-line preset restarts every 4.4 time units, so the history is not a single uniform motion).

## 7. The v6f kernel (default since v6.2)

v6f is the v6 shader source compiled with `#define F32N` (`V6F_SHADERS`, `V6F_KNOWN_PATH_SHADERS`; in the app, *Kernel: v6f*). Every change is inside `#ifdef F32N`, so the v6 and v5 programs are unchanged (tests/gpu/v6f.mjs checks v6 bit for bit against the v6 shader and app of main at commit 904bcce, which include the κ-form and Newton fixes, kept in reference/ as shader_v6_1.glsl and web_v6_1.html; this is not the v6.0.0 kernel of preprint v6.1; web_app.mjs checks v5 against the original file).

**What changes.**
1. *Chord deficit from the CPU.* For each segment the CPU forms D = 1 − |v_chord|² in float64 from the stored hi + lo knots, with the shader's chord clamp, and stores it per knot in the free channel `u_texPosLo.z` (library: `ShellHistory.emitDch`; app: the texture upload).
2. *Float32 incremental root inside the segment.* Only f₁ = R₁ − |x − z₁| and d₁ = x − z₁ at the bracketing knot are double-float. Inside the segment f(s) = f₁ + [ΔR − ℓ] + [ℓ + L₁ − L] with ℓ = |Δz|, L₁ = |d₁|, L = |d₁ − Δz|. Both brackets are non-negative and are formed without cancellation: ΔR − ℓ = ΔR·D(s)/(1 + ℓ/ΔR), where D(s), the deficit of the mean velocity over [0, s], is an affine form in per-segment deficits (cubic segments: points v_chord, −β₁, −β₂; quintic known-path segments: v_chord, Q₀, Q₁, Q₃, Q₄), and ℓ + L₁ − L = 2(d₁×Δz)²/((ℓL₁ − d₁·Δz)(ℓ + L₁ + L)) when d₁·Δz ≤ 0 (the well-conditioned denominator for the shader's time direction, R into the past), else 2(ℓL₁ + d₁·Δz)/(ℓ + L₁ + L). These are plain float32 sums of non-negative terms and need no reassociation guard; every double-float operation keeps its `BAR()`.
3. *Float32 knot bisection with a double-float bracket check.* The knot search runs in float32 on the high parts; f and d are then evaluated in double-float at both bracket knots, and the bracket steps outward if a float32 sign was wrong. In the 18 cases checked during development this gave output identical to double-float bisection (max difference 0).
4. *Field floor fix* (γ-gated). Every cross or dot product that cancels near the pulse crest (the root's d₁ × Δz, sin(β̂, n), n·a) is taken against the double-float separation d₁ with exact products, once per pixel and source: the spline is linear in (chord, b₁, b₂, acc₁, acc₂), so d₁ × z′(s) and d₁ × Δz(s) are Bernstein sums of per-segment scalars. It runs only where |u| > 4 at a knot of the segment (γ > 4.1, proper-velocity storage). `#define F32N_NOFIX` removes it.

**Corrected error attribution.** The preprint (v6.1, §2.4) attributes the remaining float32 error to the root-function arithmetic, removed by double-float evaluation of f at the knots, with everything else in float32. That is not what limits v6 at γ ≥ 71. v6's residual error there is the **float32 in-segment chord deficit**: the in-segment offset Δz and ΔR are float32 Hermite evaluations, so 1 − |Δz/ΔR|² carries a relative error ~εγ², and the root moves by ~εγ²·ΔR. The double-float knot arithmetic never removed it. In development diagnostics, v6f with the naive float32 deficit 1 − |Δz/ΔR|² came back to v6's error level, within a factor of 2 (0.62–2.05× v6), in all 18 cases (e.g. 1.05e-2 relative for both at γ = 224, M = 512, known path; validation/f32newton/f32newton_2d/results_attrib.json), while v6f with the CPU deficit reaches the interpolation level; double-float and float32 knot bisection gave identical v6f output. In Table 2 this floor dominates the v6 sweep error at γ = 224 for M ≥ 512 and at γ = 70.7 for M = 2048; at γ = 70.7, M = 512 it is part of the error (interpolation is the rest), and at M = 128 interpolation sets the error. The app's suite shows it directly: at γ = 70.7 the v6f GPU error equals the float64 evaluation of the same spline (0.0236% / 0.00607% / 0.00160% vs 0.0236% / 0.00602% / 0.00166% at M = 512 / 1024 / 2048), where v6 gives 0.0363% / 0.0158% / 0.00703% ([results/suite_swiftshader_v6_2.tsv](../results/suite_swiftshader_v6_2.tsv)).

**v6f's remaining floor**, before the field floor fix, was flat in M at about 0.6 × 3εγ (known path: 9e-6 relative at γ = 71, 2.3e-5 at γ = 224). It is float32 rounding of **directions**: near the crest n, β̂ and a are parallel to within 1/γ, so a rotation of ε in any one of them is a relative error ~εγ in 1 − n·β̂, in n·a and in the root's d₁ × Δz. Five or six stages contribute comparable amounts, so no single stage promoted to float64 helps by more than ~20%; the formula for κ, the 1/R factors and the κ-form radiation term together contribute at most a tenth (CPU emulation during development). Fix 4 above removes it: 2–17× lower known-path error, down to the float32 storage of u and acc (0.4–8e-6 relative), which it does not fix. The ring and live paths are interpolation-limited and change only within float32 noise (0.9–1.2×).

SwiftShader frame times do not follow the operation counts.


## On real hardware

The in-app suite on an Apple M1 Max (Chrome 154, ANGLE → Metal), run with the v6.0.0 kernel, reproduced the v6.0.0 pulse-sweep column to within run-to-run scatter: for example 0.0089% (γ = 22.4) and 0.059% (γ = 70.7) at M = 512; full table in [results/suite_m1max.tsv](../results/suite_m1max.tsv). Before the shader guarded every double-float operation, the same GPU gave 0.29% and 11% ([results/suite_m1max_before_fix.json](../results/suite_m1max_before_fix.json)), i.e. float32 accuracy, because the Metal compiler reassociated the error-free transforms. The v6 kernel with the κ-form radiation term and the Newton fix was measured there at commit 06edf87, before the indicator's acceleration terms and known-path mode: the in-app suite printed 0.01% and 0.03% at γ = 22.4 and 70.7, M = 512 (two decimals; GPU 0.03% / 0.02% / 0.01% at γ = 70.7, M = 512 / 1024 / 2048), and the default scene with 32 sources rendered at 62 fps (57 before those fixes; v5 120 fps, display-capped) ([results/suite_m1max_web_kform.txt](../results/suite_m1max_web_kform.txt)). The v6f kernel has not yet been measured on a real GPU.
